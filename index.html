How to use Mixdown

Quick start

  • Type the Song title once under Project. It also fills in the Title tag and the export file name.
  • Under Tracks, choose an audio file for Track 1. Add more tracks (up to 12) with Add track, or add a tone track. Got a zip of stems? Tap Open stems zip and each file becomes its own track.
  • Press Play. Tap the ruler to jump anywhere, or use the buttons to go to the start or end or skip 10 seconds. Turn on Loop to repeat a selection while you adjust it.
  • Adjust each track's volume, pan, and fades, then shape the whole mix in EQ and spectrum.
  • Under Export, press Export WAV, then tap Save. If nothing happens, press and hold Save and choose Download link.

Projects

Name your project and tap Save project, then Save. You get one .mixdown.zip file with everything: tracks, clips, edits, recordings, tones, EQs, effects, mastering settings, song details, cover art, and the audio itself. Open project brings it all back, on this device or another one. New project starts fresh.

Editing

Tap a track's waveform to make it the active track. In Select mode, drag across it to select a section. Then:

  • Cut removes it and leaves a gap; Copy keeps it. Move the playhead, tap the track you want, and Paste at playhead. That's how you move a section to another track.
  • Duplicate places a copy right after the selection, or right after the selected clip.
  • Delete and close gap removes it and slides everything after it left. Silence mutes it in place. Trim to selection keeps only the selected part of that track.

Split at playhead cuts the clip under the playhead into two clips. In Move mode, tap a clip to select it, then drag it sideways to move it. To put it on another track, pick the track in the selected clip's Track menu under the edit buttons, where you can also type its exact start time. Swipe on tracks (next to Loop) decides what touches on the tracks do: Edit (the default) makes selecting and moving reliable, including dragging a clip up or down onto another track; to scroll the page, swipe on the striped strip at the right edge of the tracks. Scroll lets up-and-down swipes anywhere over the tracks scroll the page. Tap a track's name under Tracks to open or close its settings, use Shown/Hidden to choose which tracks appear in the player (hidden tracks still play), and Undo/Redo name exactly what they change.

Clip fades and blends: select a clip in Move mode and set its Fade in and Fade out (seconds) under the edit buttons. To join it to the next clip on the same track, open Blend into next clip. Pick a style: Both blend (the ending fades out while the next part fades in), Fade out (the next part comes in at full), Fade in (the ending plays out at full), or Dip (fade out, a moment of silence, then fade in). Type the fade-out and fade-in lengths separately (0.03 or 30ms for a splice, a few seconds for a slow swell), and set the Curve from sharp to wave-like. The picture shows the two volume curves as you change them. Then tap Blend into next clip.

Exact times

Tap the time in the player to type where the playhead should go. Type only the numbers: 31425 becomes 3:14.250. Tap Exact times next to Select and Move to type a selection's start and end the same way, or set either one from the playhead, then tap Select.

Undo steps back through your last 60 edits. Edits never change your original files.

Recording

Open Recording and tap Turn on microphone. Use wired headphones so the music doesn't leak into your take; Bluetooth adds delay. Tap the track to record onto, move the playhead to where you want to start, and press ● Record at the bottom right of the player. After the count-in, sing or play along; press ■ Stop (the same button) to finish. Each take becomes its own clip, so you can split, trim, move, and duplicate it.

To stop the microphone, tap Turn off microphone or the red Mic on button in the player bar. It also turns off by itself when you leave the app, unless you're recording. Run Measure latency once per phone or mic (speaker on, no headphones). It sets the Timing fix so takes line up with the music. If a take still sounds early or late, adjust the slider and record again.

EQ

Choose what to EQ in EQ for: the whole mix or any single track, each with its own 8 bands. The EQ is locked so scrolling can't bump it; tap Edit EQ to change it, and Done when finished. All bands that are on work together. Pick a band (1 to 8) to see or edit it, and set its type, frequency, gain, and width (Q). Reset band puts one band back to its starting setting. While editing, you can also drag a band's dot on the display. The amber line shows your EQ curve over the live spectrum while the mix plays. The buttons under the display choose which spectrums to show: the whole mix (shaded) and each track (a line in its color). Each project keeps its own EQ; a new project starts with every band off at a common starting point, with a note on what that band is usually used for.

Effects

Each track has an Effects section at the bottom of its card: Noise gate, Compressor, Saturation, Chorus / flanger, Phaser, Tremolo, Echo / delay (can sync to the tempo in Recording), and Reverb (room, hall, or plate). Check an effect to turn it on and show its controls. They run in that order, after the track's EQ. Changes apply when you let go of a slider. Reverb and echo tails are kept at the end of an export instead of being cut off.

Tone tracks

Add tone track creates a generated tone you can layer under your music. Choose Binaural (a slightly different tone in each ear; needs stereo headphones), Monaural (both tones mixed, works on speakers), or Isochronic (one tone pulsing on and off; adjust how soft the pulse edges are). Set the Carrier (the tone you hear) and the Beat (how fast it pulses), or tap a band button. Glide the beat moves smoothly from one beat rate to another over the track. Add a Noise bed (white, pink, or brown) under the tone and set its level, or choose Noise only for a track of just noise. Press Update tone after changes. Tone tracks work like any other track: volume, fades, EQ, split, and trim. Pan is off for binaural so each ear keeps its own tone.

Binaural beats only work in stereo. If a phone speaker or app plays your song in mono, they disappear; monaural and isochronic tones survive.

Mastering

In Mastering, type your Target loudness (0 to -20 LUFS) and Ceiling, choose a Hard (brickwall) or Soft (rounded) limiter, and set the Look-ahead and Release. Press Master now: the app measures your mix, raises or lowers it to the target, limits the peaks, and checks again until it lands on the number. You'll see the final loudness, peak, gain added, and how much limiting it took. Turn on Listen to the master and press Play to hear it. If you change anything in the mix afterward, Listen to the master turns itself off so Play always matches what you see; master again to hear the new version. Tap Save as my defaults to keep your usual settings; every new project starts with them, and Use my defaults puts them back on any song (for example, a project whose look-ahead got bumped).

Under Export, pick a Format: WAV (lossless), M4A (smaller, 256 kbps AAC), or MP4 cover art video (your cover art, or black, with the song, square, 9:16, or 16:9, ready to post, with optional visual rows in your color: a live waveform, a slow waveform that shows beats and pulses, spectrum bars (plain, mirrored, stacked in track colors, or EQ screen: the whole mix shaded with a glowing line for each track, like the live EQ display), and a whole-song waveform with a progress marker; check any combination). Fill in Song details to save the title, artist, album, lyrics, and cover art inside the file.

Repeat and loops

In Export, Repeat plays the song 2, 3, or 5 times in one file, or fills 3 to 30 minutes, ending with a gentle fade. Turn on Blend the joins to choose how each repeat flows into the next, with the same styles as clip blends. The first play always starts clean and the last one ends naturally. Dip fades the ending out and the beginning back in, so a repeat sounds like a quiet moment in the song. With Repeat off, Blend the joins makes a single file that loops smoothly when a player repeats it; Dip keeps its very start clean. Long files are large, so M4A is a good choice for them.

Editing tags only

In Export, check Only edit the tags of a song I already have, then Open a song (WAV, MP3, M4A, or MP4). Its details and cover art fill in under Song details. Change them and tap Save tagged copy; it makes a new file with "-tagged" added to the name, without re-encoding the audio. Uncheck the box to go back to exporting your mix.

Music players show cover art in their own frame, usually a square, whatever shape the picture is. For a full-screen look, use the MP4 video export in the shape you want.

Layout

The Size buttons (100%, 85%, 70%) shrink the whole page on a phone so more fits on screen; you can still pinch to zoom in. The Layout buttons under the title switch between Auto (side by side on wide screens), 1 column, and 2 columns, whatever your screen size. Your choice is remembered.

Cleanup

Suno and other AI music often has harsh S sounds, hiss, a swirly metallic shimmer, piercing upper mids, or boomy low mids, and close, quiet vocals can have mouth clicks and P or B pops. Cleanup quiets each one only at the moments it happens, so the rest of the song stays as it was. Use the Mouth-click remover and Pop softener on a vocal track, not the whole mix. Remove end glitches (on by default) trims the split-second click or swipe that Suno stems and some other files have at the very end, after the song has faded out. Tracks that had one show a note with Put it back. Choose Clean up: the whole mix or one track (a vocal stem is the best place for the de-esser). Turn on what you hear and pick Gentle, Medium, or Strong. Cleanup works on the finished sound, so tap Hear cleanup: it renders the master, then press Play. Uncheck Listen to the master under Mastering to hear the original again.

Section fix turns the volume or treble up or down in one stretch of the song, with smooth half-second ramps in and out, for example a chorus that's too loud or an ending that gets too bright. Select the part on the timeline and tap Add section, or type the start and end.

Keep it smooth

  • Pause before big jobs. Master now, Hear cleanup, and Export reprocess the whole song. Let them work without live playback at the same time.
  • Pause while you change lots of settings, then press Play to listen. Every change during playback rebuilds the sound on the spot.
  • Close EQ and spectrum when you don't need to watch it. The live spectrum is one of the busiest things to draw.
  • Keep Mixdown on screen while a video exports. The app keeps the screen awake by itself, but switching apps pauses the video.
  • A stutter isn't a bug. Your phone is mixing every track, running every EQ, drawing the picture, and rebuilding it all live while you change things. Sometimes it has to catch up.

Good to know

  • Clean slate (in EQ and in Cleanup) turns off every EQ band and every Cleanup tool on every track at once. Volumes, fades, edits, and band settings stay, so you can turn bands back on. Undo clean slate brings it all back.
  • Settings summary (in Project) lists everything you've changed from the defaults, track by track, so you can copy it or use it as a recipe for another song.
  • Volume, pan, and gain sliders move only when you drag the circle itself, so scrolling past can't bump them. Double-tap a slider to reset it. Tap any slider's number to type an exact value, or reset it. While you drag, the number also shows just above your finger. Volumes snap to 0 dB as you pass it.
  • The player stays at the bottom of the screen. Waveforms are scaled to fit each track so quiet recordings are easy to see; Wave size enlarges them further. Neither changes the sound.
  • Insert silence (with the edit buttons) pushes everything after the playhead later on every track. A selected clip's Starts at and Ends at take minutes and seconds (1:15.2) or plain seconds; an earlier end trims the clip.
  • Your audio never leaves your device. Your current session saves itself on this device as you work; if the app closes or refreshes, it offers to Restore it next time. Use Save project for copies you want to keep or move.
  • Open the app in Chrome or Safari, not inside Instagram or Facebook, or choosing files may not work.
  • The Peak reading after export tells you if the mix clips (goes over 0 dB). Turn on mastering to keep the export from clipping.
